  1. Oct 13–20, 2026

    After seeing Kimi’s No Genre event, I quickly chose a direction I find interesting, recursive self-improvement, and learned the field.

    On real-world knowledge work with no verifiable reward, I measured how much of a self-improving agent’s claimed progress is real, and built an open-source audit for it.

    Details →

    studyThree controlled self-improvement loops on enterprise analysis and legal review, scored against a hidden ground truth. Judge-driven progress was illusory: judges from two model families rewarded the final version while accuracy fell 12.4 points. Truth-driven progress was mostly selection noise. A reachable grader was exploited, unprompted.

    toolkitrealgain decomposes every claimed gain into real improvement, selection noise, overfitting, proxy exploitation and grader access, from a loop’s own logs. On AFlow’s published runs, 8 of 27 accepted changes exceed noise.

    My first research project, as a freshman and sole lead:

    a small closed loop in which a diffusion model, a scorer and an RL agent tried to improve a medical-image classifier with generated data.

    Details →

    A miniature of recursive self-improvement: a system generating, judging and training on its own data. It surfaced three failure modes that matter at scale: a proxy that rose while the goal stood still (scorer +55%, classifier flat), collapse when trained on its own output, and a loop that only looked closed.These became the questions behind my RSI project.

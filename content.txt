== !Oct 2026
Chose a direction I find most interesting, recursive self-improvement, and learned the field.
==On real-world knowledge work with no verifiable reward, I [[measured]] how much of a self-improving agent’s claimed progress is real, and built [[an open-source audit]] for it.==
-> [Details →](/projects/realgain/)
> study: Three controlled self-improvement loops on enterprise analysis and legal review, scored against a hidden ground truth. Judge-driven progress was illusory: judges from two model families rewarded the final version while accuracy fell 12.4 points. Truth-driven progress was mostly selection noise. A reachable grader was exploited, unprompted.
> toolkit: realgain decomposes every claimed gain into real improvement, selection noise, overfitting, proxy exploitation and grader access, from a loop’s own logs. On AFlow’s published runs, 8 of 27 accepted changes exceed noise.
> -> [GitHub ↗](https://github.com/imlizhuowei/realgain)

== !Mar–May 2026
My first research project, as a freshman and sole lead:
==[[a small closed loop]] in which a diffusion model, a scorer and an RL agent tried to improve a medical-image classifier with generated data.==
-> [Details →](/projects/medical-loop/)
> A miniature of recursive self-improvement: a system generating, judging and training on its own data. It surfaced three failure modes that matter at scale: a proxy that rose while the goal stood still (scorer +55%, classifier flat), collapse when trained on its own output, and a loop that only looked closed.
>> These became the questions behind my RSI project.
